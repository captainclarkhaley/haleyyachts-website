# The Logbook, August 2026 - NOTE section

Editor's note up front: this needed very little. The voice is the whole asset here. Every change below is a repeated word, a comma, or one judgment call on the industry line. Nothing was smoothed, nothing was made more professional.

## Edited version

This month my boat insurance is due for renewal. My boat is an older Sabre, and though I've spent tons of money to make Roam beautiful, seaworthy, and trouble-free, I discovered that to the insurance companies she's just an old boat, priced accordingly. To make matters worse, in my experience insurance companies are doing everything they can to avoid paying claims. Dealing with them always puts me in a bad mood, and I have learned to counter my frustration by digging into the insurance industry and making sure I'm prepared for the worst. So I've dedicated this month's newsletter to understanding yacht insurance, the companies we have to work with, and how to be prepared for a possible accident and claim. Feel free to reach out if you're confused, or want a shoulder to cry on! Happy insuring!

## What changed and why

1. "trouble free" to "trouble-free" - hyphenated compound adjective before the noun it modifies.
2. Added a comma after "an older Sabre" - two clauses joined by "and", the comma stops the sentence running on.
3. Removed the comma after "to the insurance companies" - it was splitting the clause from its own subject.
4. "and is priced accordingly" to "priced accordingly" - drops two words, lands the sentence harder on the insult.
5. Added "in my experience" to the claims sentence - judgment call, see the note below. Every word of the original is still there.
6. "Dealing with insurance companies" to "Dealing with them" - "insurance companies" appeared three times in three consecutive sentences. This was the third.
7. Added a comma before "and I have learned" - two independent clauses.
8. "learning more about the insurance industry" to "digging into the insurance industry" - "I have learned" and "by learning" sat six words apart. "Digging into" also sounds more like a man doing it out of spite, which is the point.
9. "prepared for the worst case" to "prepared for the worst" - judgment call, minor. "The worst" is the idiom and it is one beat shorter. Revert if you like the specificity of "worst case".
10. "This month I've dedicated this newsletter" to "So I've dedicated this month's newsletter" - "This month" opened the first sentence too. "So" also does connective work the original was missing, turning the frustration into the reason for the issue.
11. "the companies that we have to work with" to "the companies we have to work with" - dropped an unnecessary "that".

Deliberately NOT changed: the comma in "if you're confused, or want a shoulder to cry on!" is technically unnecessary before "or", but it is a comic beat that sets up the punchline. It stays.

## The industry line

The only real call in here is change 5.

"Insurance companies are doing everything they can to avoid paying claims" is a flat statement of fact about an entire industry, sitting under your name and the brokerage's. Two words, "in my experience", turn it into your read on it without removing a single word of the original or softening the verb. It is arguably even better as a line: a broker who has actually fought a claim is more credible than a man making a general accusation.

If it still reads deflated to you, cut the two words and run it exactly as you wrote it. This is a light preference, not a hill.

Alternate phrasing if you want the bite sharper rather than safer: "To make matters worse, every insurer I've dealt with is doing everything it can to avoid paying claims."
